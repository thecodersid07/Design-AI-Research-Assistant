SYNTHETIC DEMO DOCUMENT — NOT PUBLISHED RESEARCH
AI tutoring: guided practice scenario. All participants, observations, and findings below are fictional examples created for ResearchLens demonstrations. This document must not be cited as empirical research.

In this fictional classroom scenario, a teacher uses an AI tutor during algebra practice. The tutor offers hints before showing an answer and asks learners to explain each step. Students in the imagined guided-practice group complete more exercises correctly during supported sessions than students using a static worksheet. The scenario illustrates a possible benefit of immediate feedback: learners can correct an incorrect step before repeating it through the rest of a problem. It does not establish that an actual product improves learning.

The fictional teacher checks a selection of tutor explanations and intervenes when hints are confusing. Students are encouraged to describe why a proposed step is valid rather than copying a final answer. Within the scenario, this combination of teacher supervision and structured prompts appears more helpful than unrestricted chatbot access. Learners describe the system as approachable because they can request repeated explanations without waiting for the whole class. These descriptions are invented for demonstration and are not interview findings from real participants.

The main limitation is that performance is observed while the tutor is available. A higher supported practice score does not establish independent understanding or long-term retention. The scenario has no randomized assignment, no delayed assessment, no validated achievement measure, and no evidence about other subjects or age groups. Teacher attention and extra time spent practicing could explain the imagined improvement. The fictional classroom also has reliable internet access, which limits what this example can say about access barriers.

A useful follow-up question would compare supervised hint-based tutoring with worksheets under equal practice time, then assess learners without assistance after a delay. Incorrect explanations, student confidence, and teacher workload would also need measurement. This proposed comparison is a design idea only; no such study was conducted for this demo. Treat this document as synthetic material for testing passage retrieval and source comparison, not as support for educational decisions.
