SYNTHETIC DEMO DOCUMENT — NOT PUBLISHED RESEARCH
AI tutoring: independent learning scenario. Every observation in this document is invented for the ResearchLens prototype. No study was conducted, and no published research is represented.

This fictional scenario describes learners who use an unrestricted AI tutor for introductory mathematics homework. They finish assignments more quickly while the system is available. However, when the imagined learners attempt unfamiliar problems without the tutor, their performance does not improve compared with their earlier unaided work. The scenario deliberately differs from a guided-practice example: access to quick answers may improve task completion without producing independent understanding. These outcomes are invented examples rather than measured effects.

Within the scenario, some learners request final solutions before trying a problem themselves. Others accept plausible explanations even when the tutor introduces an error. A fictional instructor notices that polished homework solutions can conceal gaps in students' reasoning. Learners report confidence while using the tool, but confidence is not treated as a reliable measure of mastery. The example separates satisfaction, speed, supported accuracy, and unaided transfer because these outcomes need not move together. It does not establish that all AI tutoring leads to overreliance.

Important limitations include a small imagined convenience group, no random assignment, and no controls for prior knowledge or motivation. The unfamiliar assessment problems may be harder than the original homework, so the scenario cannot attribute poor transfer solely to tutoring. The fictional observations do not compare carefully designed hints with direct answers, and they include no long-term retention assessment. Teacher practices and the quality of individual model responses are not held constant.

A future evaluation could require an initial student attempt, offer graduated hints, and include delayed unaided assessments. It could record how often learners copy answers, question explanations, or revise their own reasoning. Such measurements might distinguish productive support from dependency. This is only a proposed evaluation plan and not evidence that those safeguards work. This synthetic demo document exists to provide a contrasting source for retrieval, limitations, and evidence-reference testing. It must never be described as a real trial or cited as published research.
